# Catalyst: engineering retrospective

Catalyst was built to answer a narrow question: why did a large-cap stock move on a particular trading day? Given a ticker and session, it compares the stock's return with the market, sector and peers, then looks for news or a filing published before the move. It returns a cause only when it can point to the source's own words and show that the evidence was available in time. An answer is a claim about a past event, not a forecast or a trading signal.

## What was built

The public product is a browser workbench for replaying a featured case or analysing a covered day. The React client calls a FastAPI service, which admits and streams a run through an attribution graph. The data layer maintains daily market context, filings, headlines, article bodies and a served search corpus. The evaluation package scores saved runs offline; it does not sit in the production request path. The [README](../README.md) describes the user flow, and [Architecture](architecture.md) documents the service and package boundaries.

The published flow has four steps. Deterministic market calculations establish the session context. A researcher model proposes causes and quotes. A fact checker tests whether a quote explains this stock's move in this session. Finally, fixed editor rules validate quote fidelity, timing, subject relevance and market context before publishing. The five public outcomes distinguish a confirmed cause, a reported but unconfirmed cause, group movement, no clear cause and an ordinary move. “Models propose; code decides” captures the intended control boundary: model output is candidate material, not the published answer by itself.

## Architecture changed as evidence accumulated

The early implementation was a LangGraph Miner → Critic → Judge workflow. Miner searched a LanceDB index using BM25 and vector retrieval, combined candidates, and reranked them. Critic graded evidence, and Judge synthesized an attribution. Commits `d985849e` (Miner), `d96253b6` (Critic), `a6aa59ba` (Judge), `d429020f` (graph), and `90d84001` (LanceDB) establish the sequence in April 2026. This was a reasonable prototype for exploring retrieval and agent roles, but it gave several model stages authority close to the final decision.

The design later moved toward an evidence-constrained desk cascade: extraction and event grouping propose material, a fact checker checks same-session relevance, and deterministic gates control publication. The system retained model flexibility for reading varied language while making quote, time and subject checks explicit in code. The fact checker was added to address a concrete error class: published quotes about the wrong event. In the research-phase experiment on 36 sessions (216 runs), it cut the wrong-event rate among published causes from 37.6% to 22.5% (−15.1 points, 95% interval −27.5 to −4.5). This is evidence for the fact checker on that set. It does not establish performance on all stocks, future inputs, or every later system version.

Retrieval complexity was reduced too. Dense embeddings, rank fusion and cross-encoder reranking were removed from the serving path; SQLite FTS5 remained. In the sealed M8-D recall@8 comparison, FTS5 found 9 of 29 relevant items (0.294), dense ranking 10 of 29 (0.298), hybrid search 6 of 29 (0.183), and dense ranking with a time window 12 of 29 (0.372), at the cost of fewer primary-source hits. No arm reached the 0.75 target. A separate rerank probe scored 0.272, and the ideal candidate and display policy would have reached 0.839. These are measurements on a frozen research benchmark, not live-product recall. That margin did not justify keeping embeddings, fusion and reranking on the serving path, and the gap to the ceiling pointed at candidate generation rather than the ranker.

## Evaluation learned to distinguish answers from correctness

An early status-matching metric rewarded the system for returning an answer. It scored an always-answer baseline at 96% and Catalyst at 77%, even though those scores did not measure whether the cause matched the reference. The project replaced it with labels grounded in the expected action and answer quality. For cases that should be answered, MATCH and PARTIAL distinguish a supported full explanation from a useful but incomplete one; WRONG and MISSED separate a wrong publication from an abstention. For cases that should be abstained on, CORRECT_ABSTAIN gets credit and publishing receives an unsupported label. This change makes the evaluation reflect the product's central trade-off: an answer is not automatically better than a justified refusal.

Results were tracked across development, held-out and prospective rounds, with separate meanings. Development cases informed iteration; held-out cases tested a frozen choice; prospective rounds tested newer operation on later cases. Those results are not interchangeable. The README's table labels its numbers as research-phase results on earlier pipeline versions. The system card records additional frozen configurations and their limitations, including small samples, model-judged labels and cases that were not human-reviewed.

A November 2026 diagnosis examined 39 failures from A′, the October publisher version underlying v1.0: 16 from the held-out test set and 23 from the development set used for tuning. The corrected first-failure funnel found 13 cases without a supporting document in the candidate pool; 10 where the researcher did not propose a supporting document already in its prompt; 9 extraction misses; 3 editor-rule blocks; 3 different published causes; and 1 fact-checker rejection. The original funnel had mislabeled proposals because it looked in the wrong trace field. The correction mattered. Where a gold-supporting document was in the pool, it was in the researcher's prompt, with no pack drop or truncation, so most of those failures happened after retrieval. The 13 pool misses were not split into documents the corpus never had and documents search failed to return.

Identifiability was assessed by a model on the pool documents for those 39 failures, with no human review. Its labels were 14 clear, 17 with no pre-close evidence in the pool, 7 ambiguous, and 1 bad-data case. These are judgments about that pool and those October A′ failures; they are neither a human-validated ceiling nor a ceiling across all moves. Seven of the 39 failures hinged on same-day wires stored with a date and no time, so the timing rules could not place them before the move. That was diagnosed and left unfixed.

A replay tested an extraction prompt change against stored requests for the 19 researcher and extraction cases. Across 96 quote-check outcomes, the current prompt produced 6 usable quotes and the proposed prompt produced 4; only 2 versus 1 cases had a usable quote in at least two of three runs. The change did not help and was not merged. The research corpus behind those traces had been deleted, and the 2026-11-03 served snapshot had none of these sessions, so the replay is not an end-to-end quality result.

## Data defined the practical ceiling

The daily attribution path uses daily prices and volume, SEC 8-K material, company-news discovery and a limited set of article bodies. The data card documents provider coverage, terms and timing. It distinguishes operational sources from trial collection: collectors for minute bars, option snapshots, rating changes and several news feeds ran for a few weeks in October and November 2026 and were retired on November 12. Their stores were never read by attribution. The project did not have consensus estimates or macro expectations suitable for explaining what the market expected before a release. Some macroeconomic actual values existed locally, but the data card says they were not used by the publisher.

Data rights and availability constrained what could be stored and shown. The data card says PR Newswire and Business Wire press-release feeds were retained as metadata only—title, link, publication time, summary and identified tickers. Their article bodies were not fetched or redistributed under the documented terms. Finnhub free-tier use was for personal research; bodies from sites that prohibit automated scraping were excluded. The result was uneven evidence depth: headline discovery could be broad while usable, time-qualified body text remained sparse.

Point-in-time correctness was another constraint. Publication timestamps can be date-only, provider timestamps can have timezone errors, and the SEC submissions API time was found to disagree with the EDGAR index page for a substantial issuer subset. The system card describes corrections and their audited effects, but also makes clear that previous evaluation figures were not all recalculated. A timestamp error can move an item into the wrong trading window; a correct event found after the close is not valid evidence for an earlier price move. These are data and provenance issues that prompt tuning cannot repair.

## What remains hard

Some moves have no clear public evidence before the close. Others have several plausible simultaneous causes—company news, sector repricing, commodities or macro releases—and a single-session label may itself oversimplify the event. Even when a source exists, it may arrive late, have a date without a time, or be unavailable under usable terms. Correct abstention is therefore an intended outcome, but it also limits coverage. A multi-day narrative built from single-day answers would inherit those gaps and could make a smooth story sound more certain than its underlying evidence.

## Why development is paused

Pausing feature work is a scope decision. The remaining credible quality gains appear to depend mostly on document coverage, timeliness, point-in-time history and licensing. The proposed multi-day analysis would also need market expectations and macro context that the project does not have in a production-ready form. Those dependencies are larger than another agent or prompt iteration, and they would require new data access, evaluation design and maintenance.

The surrounding tools also changed. In November 2026 TradingView shipped AI Copilot inside its charting platform. According to its help center, the assistant works directly with TradingView's quotes, indicators, financials, news, earnings, economic calendars and screener data, and it can act on the chart; TradingView also publishes an MCP server with 38 tools over the same kinds of data. That covers much of the interactive research workflow a multi-day Catalyst was meant to support. TradingView has not published an agent design or an evaluation, and its documentation does not describe pre-move evidence windows, verbatim citations or rule-based abstention, so the two are not comparable on attribution quality. Its terms license the data for display and bar using the content, including model output, to train or test an AI system, so that data cannot feed this corpus or this evaluation.

Further progress would mostly mean buying, licensing and maintaining data, with little left to learn from the system's design. For a solo developer that is a poor trade at this stage, and leaving Catalyst as a finished reference is the proportionate choice.

## Lessons that transfer

- **Measure the behavior users need.** The always-answer score looked strong until gold-label metrics separated correctness from the act of answering.
- **Make the publication boundary deterministic.** The fact checker reduced wrong-event publication on its experiment set, and quote, timing and subject gates in code kept every published answer checkable.
- **Benchmark added complexity against a simple baseline.** FTS5 was close to dense retrieval and ahead of the hybrid and reranked runs, and no arm came near the ceiling, so the effort went to candidate coverage instead.
- **Trace the whole failure path.** Correcting the funnel exposed extraction misses and candidate-pool gaps that an imprecise “retrieval failure” label hid.
- **Treat data provenance as product behavior.** Date-only wires and timestamp corrections changed which evidence could safely support an answer.

## Status

Catalyst v1.0 is released and public. Active feature development has stopped; the repository, demo cases and research results remain available as reference material. Issues are welcome; feature work has stopped; bug, security and documentation fixes may be reviewed slowly. The live demo remains linked from the README; its availability is operational and can change independently of this repository.
